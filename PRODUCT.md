# Product

Rotom Dex: a fan-made, interactive 3D Rotom Dex that looks and behaves like the living character from the Pokémon anime and games, and knows more than the one in the series did. Written from the owner's build brief and follow-up notes; not yet reviewed line by line with them.

## Platform

web

## Stack

Vite, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS v4, Three.js, React Three Fiber, drei, postprocessing (bloom), TanStack Query. Static deployment.

## Users

Pokémon fans, on a phone or a desktop, who want to look something up and enjoy the character while they do: a species, a berry, a move, a gym badge.

## Product Purpose

Make looking things up feel like asking Rotom. It scans, reacts with its whole body, shows the answer on its own screen and announces it in its own voice. Breadth is the second goal: it should carry everything about the Pokémon world that data exists for, not only species.

## Positioning

Successor to the owner's earlier Pokédex builds (a sprite Pokédex, then a 3D Pokédex device). The device became a character.

## Operating Context

Casual and playful, often with sound on. Speech and sound need a first tap before browsers allow them. Lookups depend on PokéAPI, so "scanning", "not found" and "connection lost" are normal states.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The Pokémon databank covers every species and every alternate form PokéAPI holds (Mega, Gigantamax, regional and the rest), each with shiny artwork where it exists.
- Ten databanks: Pokémon, berries, snacks, items, Poké Balls, moves, abilities, types, regions and badges, natures. Five more are reserved as empty slots.
- Pokémon, item, badge and type-label images come from PokéAPI; there are no 3D Pokémon models.
- PokéAPI has no gym data. Which badge belongs to which leader, town and region is a hand-kept dataset.
- Rotom's model and face artwork are generated by scripts in the repo, drawn to follow the official design. The phone form is built from HTML and CSS and loads no 3D code.
- Rotom's voice is a set of recorded clips made by the owner. Nothing is synthesised in the browser: the owner judged that no browser voice could be made to match, so entries are announced, then shown as text, not read aloud.
- No backend, no accounts.

## Brand Commitments

Pinned by the owner:

- Rotom must look like the actual Rotom Dex from the series, as close to one-to-one as possible. The anime is the reference for its shape; its smile is a compact grin, not big block teeth.
- Every visit and refresh starts with Rotom asleep; the user powers it on.
- On a phone Rotom takes a phone form (the phone is its body) with the same functions, and it also has to be woken.
- Data is shown in Rotom itself, on its screen, not in panels beside it. Text boxes for what Rotom says are welcome.
- The interface should feel like it came out of the Pokémon world, and must not read as generic AI-generated design.
- Cel-shaded 3D with clean outlines and an electric glow; a glass tray for the keys with blue and yellow accents and lit button states; the games' own type labels.
- Rotom's four emotions and their colours are fixed: IDLE soft amber, SCANNING electric cyan, SUCCESS neon green, ERROR hazard red.

## Evidence on Hand

The owner's written spec for the state machine, animations, lighting, HUD and speech script, and their corrections during the build. Reference for Rotom's design: the anime frame and the phone wallpaper the owner supplied, Bulbapedia's Rotom Pokédex article, the Sun and Moon model sheet and official artwork.

## Product Principles

- Rotom is the interface. If something can be shown on its screen or said by it, it is not put anywhere else.
- Every lookup gets a full reaction: body, light, sky, face, voice.
- Rotom says the description; the screen shows the facts. Nothing is said twice.
- A new kind of data should be one module, not a redesign.
- Never fake data: what is not built is shown as an empty slot.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard access to every action, visible focus, 4.5:1 text contrast, status changes announced to screen readers, reduced-motion support, voice off by choice and remembered. The screen's contents are real HTML text, readable by assistive technology, not pixels in the 3D scene.
